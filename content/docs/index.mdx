This is a from-scratch course on how AI actually works, structured like a
docs site rather than a textbook. Each lesson builds on the last:

1. [History & Landscape](/docs/history-and-landscape) — how the field got here
2. [ML Fundamentals](/docs/ml-fundamentals) — learning as optimization
3. [Probability & Statistics Foundations](/docs/probability-and-statistics-foundations) — the math loss functions are built on
4. [Neural Networks & Backprop](/docs/neural-networks-and-backprop) — the algorithm behind training
5. [Tooling & The Dev Stack](/docs/tooling-and-the-dev-stack) — languages, frameworks, what you'd actually touch
6. [Computer Vision](/docs/computer-vision) — convolutions and CNNs
7. [Attention & Transformers](/docs/attention-and-transformers) — the architecture behind modern AI
8. [Generative Models](/docs/generative-models) — GANs, VAEs, diffusion — generating new content
9. [LLMs](/docs/llms) — tokenization, pretraining, fine-tuning, RLHF
10. [Reinforcement Learning](/docs/reinforcement-learning) — learning from reward, the mechanism behind RLHF
11. [Inference & Serving](/docs/inference-and-serving) — what happens when you hit "send"
12. [Evaluation & Benchmarks](/docs/evaluation-and-benchmarks) — how models are actually scored
13. [Prompt Engineering](/docs/prompt-engineering) — shaping behavior for free, no training involved
14. [RAG & Vector Databases](/docs/rag-and-vector-databases) — giving a model information it wasn't trained on
15. [Agents & Tool Use](/docs/agents-and-tool-use) — giving a model the ability to act
16. [AI Security](/docs/ai-security) — prompt injection, jailbreaks, and defending a deployed system from misuse
17. [Applied & Agentic Systems](/docs/applied-and-agentic-systems) — how the patterns above combine

## Who this is for

You should be comfortable with basic programming and high-school math
(functions, vectors, derivatives at an intuitive level). No prior ML
background is assumed — each lesson introduces its own vocabulary before
using it.

## How to read it

Lessons are meant to be read in order — later ones assume the vocabulary
and intuition built in earlier ones.

Three things to know about the format:

- **Technical terms link to the [reference](/reference)**, a glossary of
  short standalone definitions. Follow them when a word is new; skip them
  when it isn't.
- **Interactive widgets** let you manipulate the thing being described —
  drag a learning rate around, step through backprop, watch attention
  weights change.
- **Deep dives are collapsed by default.** Anything marked *Under the hood*
  contains the formal math and edge cases. The main text is complete
  without them, so ignore them on a first pass and open them on a second.
  The toggle at the top of each lesson opens them all at once.
