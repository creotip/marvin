The **Perceptron** (1958, Frank Rosenblatt) was the first system built
from an artificial neuron that could actually *learn* its weights from
labeled examples, rather than being hand-programmed — a direct, if
primitive, ancestor of the [gradient descent](/reference/gradient-descent)
training loop used throughout modern [deep learning](/reference/deep-learning).
In 1969, Marvin Minsky and Seymour Papert's book *Perceptrons* proved a
single-layer perceptron can't learn certain simple functions (like XOR),
which stalled connectionist research funding for over a decade — until
[backpropagation](/reference/backpropagation) provided the fix for
multi-layer networks.
