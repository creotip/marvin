title: Andrew Ng

---

**Andrew Ng** (b. 1976) has arguably done more than anyone to make machine
learning teachable outside a research lab. His Stanford CS229 course and
its Coursera adaptation, first launched in 2011, introduced millions of
students to [gradient descent](/reference/gradient-descent),
[regularization](/reference/regularization), and the practical craft of
building models — often as their literal first exposure to the field. He
co-founded Coursera itself in 2012 with Daphne Koller, betting that
university-quality courses could be delivered online at essentially
unlimited scale.

On the research and industry side, Ng co-founded Google Brain in 2011,
where large-scale unsupervised learning experiments (including a famous
2012 result where a neural network learned to recognize cats from
unlabeled YouTube video frames, without ever being told what a cat was)
demonstrated that scaling deep learning on distributed hardware could
uncover meaningful structure without hand-labeled data. He later served
as Chief Scientist at Baidu, leading its AI Group, and founded Landing AI
and DeepLearning.AI, both focused on applying machine learning in
industrial and educational settings respectively.

Ng has been a consistent voice for the "AI as the new electricity"
framing — that machine learning is a general-purpose technology set to
transform most industries the way electrification did — and for
prioritizing practical, applied AI education over purely theoretical
research.
