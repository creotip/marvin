**Demis Hassabis** (b. 1976) was a chess prodigy and video game designer
before co-founding DeepMind in 2010 with a stated mission of "solving
intelligence, and then using that to solve everything else." The lab's
first major result, AlphaGo, combined deep neural networks with
[reinforcement learning](/docs/reinforcement-learning) and tree search to
beat the world's top Go players in 2016 — a game long assumed to be
decades away from falling to computers because of its vast search space
and reliance on pattern recognition rather than brute-force calculation.

DeepMind's most consequential result came later. AlphaFold, released in
2020 and open-sourced in 2021, predicted 3D protein structures from amino
acid sequences at a level of accuracy that essentially solved a
50-year-old grand challenge in biology, and made its predictions for
nearly all catalogued proteins freely available to researchers worldwide.
Hassabis and DeepMind colleague John Jumper shared the 2024 Nobel Prize in
Chemistry for the work — one of the very few Nobel Prizes awarded
specifically for an AI system's output.

After Google merged DeepMind with its Brain team in 2023, Hassabis took
over as CEO of the combined Google DeepMind, now responsible for the
Gemini family of models — putting the same organization behind board-game
superhuman play, scientific discovery, and frontier [LLMs](/reference/llm).
