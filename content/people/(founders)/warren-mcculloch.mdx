title: Warren McCulloch
description: Neurophysiologist who, with Walter Pitts, proposed the first mathematical model of a neuron in 1943 — the ancestor of every neural network built since.
---

**Warren McCulloch** (1898–1969) was a neurophysiologist who wanted to know
whether the brain could be described in the language of logic. Working with
[Walter Pitts](/people/walter-pitts), he published "A Logical Calculus of
the Ideas Immanent in Nervous Activity" in 1943 — a paper that modeled a
neuron as a simple threshold unit: it fires if enough of its inputs are
active, otherwise it doesn't. There was no learning rule and no data, just
a claim that networks of these units could, in principle, compute anything
a Turing machine could.

That claim is the entire premise behind the [neural network](/reference/neural-network),
decades before anyone trained one. The McCulloch-Pitts neuron directly
inspired [Frank Rosenblatt](/people/frank-rosenblatt)'s
[perceptron](/reference/perceptron) a decade later, and the
threshold-then-fire structure is still recognizable in every activation
function used today.

McCulloch later organized the Macy Conferences on cybernetics, the
interdisciplinary meetings where biologists, mathematicians, and engineers
first argued in the same room about minds, machines, and feedback — a
direct precursor to the 1956 Dartmouth workshop that gave the field its
name.
