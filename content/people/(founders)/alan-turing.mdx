title: Alan Turing
description: Mathematician who formalized what "computation" means and, in 1950, asked whether a machine could think — the question the entire field still answers to.
---

**Alan Turing** (1912–1954) defined computation itself before there was
much to compute. His 1936 paper on what became known as the Turing machine
gave a precise, mathematical answer to "what can a machine calculate,
given unlimited time and memory" — the theoretical floor every real
computer, and every model trained on one, sits on. During the Second World
War he led the team that broke the German Enigma cipher at Bletchley Park,
turning that theory into working machinery under wartime pressure.

His most cited contribution to AI itself came in 1950, with the paper
"Computing Machinery and Intelligence." Rather than trying to define
intelligence, Turing proposed a test: if a human judge, conversing by
text with a machine and a person, can't reliably tell which is which, the
machine passes. The Turing test reframed the entire question — not "does
this system think" but "does its behavior make the difference
undetectable" — and that framing is still the implicit standard critics
reach for when arguing about whether an [LLM](/reference/llm)
"understands" anything.

Turing died in 1954, five years before the term
[artificial intelligence](/reference/ai) was coined at the Dartmouth
workshop he never lived to see.
