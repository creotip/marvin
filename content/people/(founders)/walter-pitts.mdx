title: Walter Pitts
description: Self-taught logician who, with Warren McCulloch, co-authored the 1943 paper modeling neurons as logic gates — the mathematical starting point for every neural network.
---

**Walter Pitts** (1923–1969) was largely self-taught, having left an
unhappy home as a teenager and taught himself formal logic from library
books. He met [Warren McCulloch](/people/warren-mcculloch) in Chicago in
the early 1940s, and the two spent nights working out whether the
all-or-nothing firing of a biological neuron could be described with the
same propositional logic Pitts had taught himself. The result, published
in 1943, was the McCulloch-Pitts neuron: a unit that sums weighted inputs
and fires if the sum clears a threshold.

The paper proved, mathematically, that networks of these simple units
could represent any logical function — the first formal argument that a
machine built from artificial neurons could in principle compute anything
a brain could. It had no notion of learning from data; the weights were
set by hand to encode a desired logical function. That piece came later,
from [Frank Rosenblatt](/people/frank-rosenblatt)'s
[perceptron](/reference/perceptron), but the basic unit — weighted
sum, threshold, fire — is still recognizable in every
[neural network](/reference/neural-network) trained today.
