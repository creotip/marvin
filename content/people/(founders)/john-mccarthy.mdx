title: John McCarthy
description: Coined the term "artificial intelligence" in 1955, organized the field's founding 1956 Dartmouth workshop, and invented Lisp.

**John McCarthy** (1927–2011) coined the phrase "artificial intelligence"
in a 1955 proposal for a summer research project at Dartmouth College —
deliberately choosing a neutral name over alternatives like "cybernetics,"
which was already associated with a different research group. The 1956
Dartmouth workshop he organized with [Claude Shannon](/people/claude-shannon),
[Marvin Minsky](/people/marvin-minsky), and Nathaniel Rochester is
conventionally treated as the field's founding event, even though it
produced no breakthroughs itself — mostly a room of people agreeing the
question was worth a name and a research program.

McCarthy's more lasting technical contribution was Lisp, a programming
language he designed in 1958 built around symbolic expressions and
recursive functions. Lisp became the dominant language of symbolic AI
research for three decades — the expert systems and logic-based reasoning
that defined the field before statistical machine learning took over — and
introduced ideas (garbage collection, conditionals as expressions,
functions as data) that show up in most modern languages.

He remained a committed advocate of symbolic, logic-based AI throughout
his career, a position that put him philosophically at odds with the
connectionist approach that would eventually dominate the field.
