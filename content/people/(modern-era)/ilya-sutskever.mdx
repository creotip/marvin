title: Ilya Sutskever
description: Co-authored AlexNet as a student, then sequence-to-sequence learning, then co-founded OpenAI and helped drive the bet that scale would produce GPT-level language models.
---

**Ilya Sutskever** was a graduate student under
[Geoffrey Hinton](/people/geoffrey-hinton) when he co-authored
[AlexNet](/reference/alexnet) in 2012 alongside Alex Krizhevsky — the
result that restarted deep learning as the field's dominant paradigm. Two
years later, with Oriol Vinyals and Quoc Le, he co-authored the
sequence-to-sequence learning paper that showed a single neural network
could map an input sequence directly to an output sequence — the
architecture that made neural machine translation practical and a direct
conceptual ancestor of how modern [LLMs](/reference/llm) handle variable-
length input and output.

In 2015 Sutskever co-founded OpenAI and became its chief scientist,
where he was among the strongest internal advocates for the bet that
scaling up [transformer](/reference/transformer)-based language models —
more data, more parameters, more compute — would keep producing
qualitatively better capabilities rather than diminishing returns. That
bet is what produced the GPT series and, later,
[RLHF](/reference/rlhf)-tuned ChatGPT, the release most credited with
moving [LLMs](/reference/llm) into mainstream use.

Sutskever left OpenAI in 2024 to found Safe Superintelligence Inc., a
lab focused specifically on alignment research ahead of capability
research — a framing that puts him, alongside
[Yoshua Bengio](/people/yoshua-bengio), among the more safety-focused
voices to emerge from within the labs pursuing frontier scale.
